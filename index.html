Anatomy of a Drought · Conclusion

The most resilient biome
on a warming planet

After surveying Earth's major biomes through two decades of satellite data, drought indices, and ecological records, one biome stands out as disproportionately resilient to climate-driven water stress: the Temperate Rainforest.

Our Verdict

Temperate Rainforest

[PLACEHOLDER] The temperate rainforest biome — found in the Pacific Northwest, Chilean Patagonia, and New Zealand's South Island — consistently records the lowest drought severity scores in the PDSI dataset (2001–2023), averaging above −1.0 throughout the observation period while most biomes plunged below −2.5. Its resilience stems from persistent maritime moisture, deep water-holding soils, and a closed-canopy structure that moderates soil temperature and reduces evapotranspiration stress.

Important Caveats

[PLACEHOLDER] Resilience to drought does not mean immunity to all climate impacts. Temperate rainforests face threats from coastal fog reduction, pest outbreaks (e.g. bark beetle), and wildfire risk at their margins. Their very rarity (<1% of land) limits habitability for large populations. This ranking reflects biome-level drought resilience based on 2001–2023 observational data — individual locations within any biome will vary significantly.
